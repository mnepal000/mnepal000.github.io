26 August 2026 · Rasuwa, Nepal

Seven hours down the river

A mountainside collapses at 8:40 in the morning. By nightfall, a wall of mud and boulders has traveled two river systems and rewritten the map. This is the flood, hour by hour.

Scroll ↓
A reconstruction in nine movements

The river stops being a river

What follows is drawn from river-gauge records, the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology, and the reporters who walked the valley afterward. Keep scrolling. The water is already moving.

I. The collapse · 08:40

The mountain falls

About two square kilometers of rock wall and glacier ice tear off Langtang Lirung and fall 1,400 meters into the Lhende Khola. Seismometers read the impact as an earthquake, magnitude 4.4, later revised to 5.2. The US Geological Survey concludes there was no earthquake at all. The shaking was the mountain falling.

I. The collapse · 08:50

The gauge goes silent

Ten minutes later, the monitoring station at Bhote Koshi-Syafrubesi stops transmitting. Its last reading: a river level of 1.62 meters. What is coming down the valley no longer resembles water.

“It wasn't really water. It was gas-like, all black and it moved like boiling water.”Yangee Tamang, watching from a helicopter over Syabrubesi
I. The collapse · 09:15

The warning

The Department of Hydrology and Meteorology confirms a major flood wave and warns everyone downstream. By now the slurry of mud, boulders, and splintered forest is moving at nearly 190 kilometers an hour, running in places 80 meters above the river's normal level.

II. Down the valley · 10:28

Galchhi

One hour and thirteen minutes after the warning, the wave reaches Galchhi on the Trishuli. Bridges, riverside settlements, and hydropower intakes in its path have minutes, not hours.

II. Down the valley · 11:50

Malekhu

The wave reaches Malekhu, the busy highway town where the Prithvi Highway meets the river. Three hours and ten minutes have passed since the mountain fell. Downstream, nobody has yet seen the worst of it.

II. Down the valley · 13:00

Mugling

The wave reaches Mugling, the junction town where highways and rivers meet. It has been traveling for over four hours and shows no sign of weakening, grinding everything in the river corridor to slurry as it goes.

III. The confluence · 15:20

Devghat

The wave reaches Devghat, the holy confluence town. Forty minutes later, at 16:00, instruments record the peak: about 5,850 cubic meters of water, mud, and debris passing every second. Nearly seven hours after a mountainside collapsed in Rasuwa, the flood is still a flood.

Nine days after the flood, rescuers heard voices inside the tunnel of the 60-megawatt Upper Trishuli-3A hydropower project and pulled two men alive from about 170 meters inside: Sanjay Sah, 30, a mechanical foreman from Saptari, and Kabir Maharjan, 45, a mechanical supervisor from Kathmandu. Sah had reportedly stayed behind in the control room to help others escape. The next day, a Nepali Army team working with South Korea's disaster response unit pulled a Chinese worker alive from an audit tunnel at Upper Trishuli-1.

Shiva Giri, a technical officer at the Mailung Hydropower project, ran with seven friends for about twelve hours when the flood hit, a dog at their heels, and spent the night in a cave before a Nepali Army helicopter lifted them out. He lost his cousin and his friends. He told RSS afterward that it would have been worse if the mountain had fallen at night, when everyone was asleep. The mountain fell at 8:40 in the morning.

Method and caveats

This is a schematic reconstruction, not a surveyed map: the river path, distances, and terrain are illustrative. Waypoint arrival times come from the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology via the wave-timeline dataset. Casualty figures are confirmed dead and reported missing as of 15 September 2026 (OCHA SitRep 8) and kept rising afterward. Survivor accounts are from RSS/Gothalo, Nepali Times via Global Voices, and People's Review reporting, as cited in the full story.

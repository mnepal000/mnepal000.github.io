About Nepal's Data

Evidence first, narrative always.

Nepal's Data is curated by Mukunda Nepal, a researcher and communications specialist based in the Washington, DC area, working at the intersection of technology, safety, and public policy. Previously with UNICEF, Freedom House, and the International Rescue Committee, he now works on AI safety. He built this portal to turn scattered public datasets on migration, the economy, democracy, media, and the environment into stories anyone can understand, with interactive charts anyone can explore.

Our method

Every story follows the same rules:

1. Real sources. We use data from official statistics offices, central banks, election commissions, and reputable international databases. Each story names them.

2. Reproducible charts. Every visualization links to the exact CSV file it was built from. Nothing is drawn by hand.

3. Plain language. We write for a curious reader, not a statistician. Methods and caveats are explained in the open, not buried in footnotes.

4. Corrections welcome. Data work has errors. When we find one, we fix it publicly and note the change.

Coverage

We start with Nepal, where open data is scattered and underused, and expand across South Asia: Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka. Topics include labor migration, remittances, elections and federalism, press freedom, digital safety, climate, and public health.

Contribute

Nepal's Data is built to be collaborative. You can:

Suggest a story. Tell us which dataset or issue deserves a story.

Share a dataset. If you have cleaned data on a covered topic, we will document and publish it with credit.

Report an error. If a number looks wrong, tell us and we will investigate.

Reuse policy

Charts and datasets may be reused with credit to Nepal's Data and the original source named alongside the data. Stories themselves may be quoted with attribution.
